Solar Generator for Home Backup

The average US customer lost about 11 hours of power in 2024, most of it from hurricanes and storms. A battery backup keeps the fridge cold, the Wi-Fi on and the lights working, with no fumes in the house. Here is how to size one.

Why outage backup matters more now

According to the U.S. Energy Information Administration, the average US customer lost about 11 hours of power in 2024. About 9 of those hours came from major events like Hurricanes Beryl, Helene and Milton. From 2014 to 2023, major events caused about 4 hours a year.

Averages hide the bad cases. Customers in South Carolina lost nearly 53 hours in 2024.

The clock that matters most is in your kitchen. The USDA says a refrigerator keeps food safe for about 4 hours without power. A full freezer holds for up to 48 hours, and a half-full one for about 24, if the doors stay closed. A battery backup turns those deadlines into non-issues.

A typical essentials day is fridge, Wi-Fi, lights, phones and a little TV: about 2,000 to 2,700 Wh. Add a CPAP or a fan and you are near 3,000 Wh.

Check the start-up rating on pumps. Sump pumps, well pumps and fridges pull two to three times their running watts for a moment when they start. Compare that number with the unit's surge rating before you buy, and check whether a well pump needs 240 volts.

Connecting it safely

The simple way: plug in directly

Run a heavy-duty extension cord from the unit to the fridge, and plug lamps, the router and chargers straight into the unit. No electrician needed.

The whole-circuit way: a transfer switch

To power your home's own outlets and hard-wired loads, you need a transfer switch installed by a licensed electrician. It keeps your system separate from the utility lines.

Never backfeed through a wall outlet

Plugging a generator into a household outlet to feed power backward into the house is dangerous to you and to the line workers repairing the grid. Do not do it with any generator, battery or gas.

Why battery beats gas indoors

A battery unit makes no exhaust, so it can sit in the kitchen. The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission says more than 80 people die each year from carbon monoxide from portable gas generators, and that gas units belong outdoors, at least 20 feet from the house.

What portable home backup will not do

It will not run central air conditioning, electric heat, an electric water heater or an electric range for long. Each of those can use more in a few hours than a portable unit holds.

If you need the whole house running through multi-day outages, look at a permanent standby system or a full home battery installed by a licensed contractor. A portable unit is the affordable answer for the essentials.

Common questions

How long will a solar generator run a refrigerator?

Divide the unit's watt-hours by the fridge's daily use. A typical fridge uses 1,000 to 1,600 Wh a day, so a 1,070 Wh unit runs one for most of a day, and a 2,048 Wh unit for about a day and a half.

How do I keep it ready between outages?

Store it charged, indoors, and check the level every few months. Charge it fully whenever a storm is in the forecast.

Can it run a sump pump?

Often, yes, if its surge rating covers the pump's start-up kick. Pumps run in short bursts, so the energy cost is usually modest. Check the pump's label for running and starting watts.

Can a solar generator power my whole house?

Not a typical one through a long outage. It is best for the essentials: fridge, freezer, Wi-Fi, lights, phones, medical devices and a pump.
