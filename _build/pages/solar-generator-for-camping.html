Why campers like a solar generator

The big reason is noise. A battery unit makes almost none, so you can run a fan or a CPAP all night without bothering the site next door.

That matters because gas generators are often restricted. Theodore Roosevelt National Park allows them from 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. Lassen Volcanic National Park allows them only in three two-hour windows a day and bans them in tent-only areas. Your campground will have its own rules, so check before you go.

The other reasons are simpler. No fuel to haul, no fumes in the tent, and you can recharge from the sun while you hike.

Count your watt-hours for the whole trip

A watt-hour (Wh) is one watt used for one hour. A 5-watt lantern on for 4 hours uses 20 Wh. Add up what you use in a day, then multiply by the days you will be out.

Watts versus watt-hours. The coffee maker pulls far more power than the cooler, but only for ten minutes, so it uses less energy over the day. Watts tell you whether a unit can start something. Watt-hours tell you how long you can keep going.

The CPAP humidifier is the big one. The heated humidifier can more than triple a CPAP's nightly use. If you can sleep without it on a short trip, or use the 12-volt DC cord many CPAP makers sell, the same battery lasts much longer.

Five camp habits that stretch your battery

Charge it full at home

Leave with 100 percent from the wall. Solar at camp then adds to a full tank instead of filling an empty one.

Use the USB and 12-volt ports

Phones, lights and 12-volt coolers run straight from those ports. Going through the AC outlet wastes power on conversion, so turn AC off when nothing needs it.

Chase the sun with the panel

Point the panel at the sun and move it as the shade moves. A panel in partial shade can lose most of its output.

Keep the unit in the shade

The panel wants sun. The battery does not. Keep the power station shaded and out of a hot car.

Top up on the drive

Most Jackery units charge from a car's 12-volt socket. It is slow, but a few hours of driving between sites adds real energy for the next night.

What a camping solar generator will not do

It will not run an electric heater through a cold night. A 1,500-watt heater empties even a 2,000 Wh unit in a little over an hour. Bring the right sleeping bag instead.

It will not cool a tent or camper overnight with an air conditioner, and it will not refill quickly under heavy clouds or tree cover. Plan for those days by bringing more battery than you think you need.

Common questions

What size solar generator do I need for camping?

For phones, lights and a speaker, about 300 Wh covers a weekend. Add a 12-volt cooler or a CPAP and you want about 1,000 Wh. Families running a fan, a cooler and a coffee maker do best with about 2,000 Wh.

Can I run a CPAP on a solar generator while camping?

Yes. Without the heated humidifier a CPAP uses roughly 240 Wh over 8 hours. With the humidifier and heated tube it can reach about 800 Wh. Turning the humidifier off, or using the 12-volt DC cord, stretches the battery much further.

Can I use it during quiet hours?

A battery unit makes almost no noise, so it does not disturb neighbors the way a gas generator does. Gas generator hours are often restricted, so check your own campground's rules.

Can I charge it from my car?

Yes. Most Jackery units charge from a car's 12-volt socket. It is slow but useful between sites.
