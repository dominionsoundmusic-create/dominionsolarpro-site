What a solar generator actually is

A solar generator is a big rechargeable battery with wall outlets, USB ports and a 12-volt socket built in, sold together with folding solar panels. Jackery calls the battery alone a portable power station. Add panels and it becomes a solar generator.

It does not burn fuel, so it makes no fumes and almost no noise. You can use it indoors. You can charge it from the sun, a wall outlet or your car.

The panels are optional. Plenty of people buy the power station alone for outage backup, charge it from the wall, and add panels later.

The five numbers that decide it

1. Capacity, in watt-hours (Wh)

How much energy the battery holds. This decides how long things run. A 1,000 Wh unit runs a 100-watt load for about 10 hours, less a little for conversion losses.

2. Output and surge, in watts (W)

Output is how much power it can push at once. Surge is the short burst it can give when a motor starts. A fridge, pump or power tool can pull two to three times its running watts for a moment at start-up. If the surge rating is too low, the unit shuts off.

3. Battery chemistry and cycles

Current Jackery models use lithium iron phosphate (LiFePO4), rated around 4,000 cycles to 70 percent capacity. At one full charge a day that is roughly ten years. Older lithium-ion packs were rated far lower, so check this line when you compare used or older units.

4. Solar input limit

The most panel wattage the unit can accept. Extra panels beyond this number charge nothing. It also sets how fast you refill from the sun.

5. Weight, or room to grow

A unit you carry to a campsite should be one you can lift. A unit for home backup can be heavy, and it helps if you can add battery packs later instead of buying a second unit.

What no portable solar generator will do

It will not run central air conditioning or electric heat for a whole house through the night. It will not refill quickly under heavy clouds, which is often exactly when the power is out. And it will not replace a permanent rooftop system for living fully off the grid.

What it will do is keep the things that matter running quietly, with no fuel and no fumes, for as long as your battery and your sun allow.

Common questions

Is a solar generator better than a gas generator?

For quiet, indoor-safe power for electronics, a fridge and a few appliances, yes. It makes no fumes and needs no fuel. A gas generator still wins when you need large amounts of power for many days with little sun, because you can keep refilling the tank.

How long does it take to charge from solar panels?

Divide the battery's watt-hours by your panel watts, then allow about 25 percent for losses. A 2,042 Wh unit on 400 watts of panels needs about 6.8 peak sun hours, roughly a day and a half of good sun in most of the US. From a wall outlet, most current Jackery units refill in about two hours or less.

Can a solar generator run a refrigerator?

Yes. A full-size fridge typically uses 1,000 to 1,600 Wh a day. A 2,000 Wh unit runs one for about a day and a half on its own, and longer with panels feeding it.
